Backstreet Boys Biography

The story of the best-selling boy band ever.

The Backstreet Boys are the best-selling boy band ever, with over 130 million records sold worldwide. Formed in Orlando, Florida in 1993, it became a sociocultural phenomenon that defined pop music in the late 1990s and early 2000s.

The band was conceived by American businessman Lou Pearlman, who tried to create a youth vocal group inspired by the success of New Kids on the Block. The first to audition were AJ McLean and Howie Dorough, who already knew each other. Later, a 13-year-old Nick Carter joined. The fourth member was Kevin Richardson, who suggested his cousin Brian Littrell to complete the band on April 20th, 1993. The name came from the Backstreet Market, a popular teen meeting place in Orlando.

Unlike other groups, their massive success first started in Europe, Canada and Asia with their self-titled debut album in 1996. The following year, they released “Backstreet’s Back” to the international market, alongside a version adapted for the United States, propelled by singles such as “Everybody (Backstreet’s Back)” and “As Long as You Love Me.” In 1999, they became global superstars. Their album “Millennium” sold over 1.1 million copies in its first week in the U.S. alone and featured their most iconic hit: “I Want It That Way.” In 2000, another album, called “Black & Blue”, solidified their status, supported by a massive world tour and tracks like “Shape of My Heart.”

In 2002, after years of intense touring, AJ McLean’s recovery from addiction, and tensions with their original management, the group took a temporary break to pursue individual projects until 2004. Two years later, Kevin Richardson left the group on good terms to focus on his family. As a quartet, the band released “Unbreakable” in 2007 and “This Is Us” two years after that. Kevin officially returned in 2012.

In 2019, they released their tenth studio album, “DNA”, which debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard 200 chart, demonstrating their enduring presence in the industry after a career spanning more than 25 years.
